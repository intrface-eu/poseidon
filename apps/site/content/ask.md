# What we need

Poseidon needs farms, scientists, engineers and money. This page lists every partner and funding type the project will need through its first three stages, what each one gets, and what we ask of them. Write to basic@intrface.eu about any line.

## Partners

### Pilot shellfish farms in Lim bay

What you get: the first units on your lines at no cost to you; a record of when and where seabream feed on your stock, by night and day, across a season; the recordings and reports from your site; a say in what we build next; your farm named as a founding pilot site, if you want that.

What we need from you: written access to your concession for listening only; one or two lines where a unit can hang for a season; a person we can call; boat access on install and service days; your own records of losses, seeding dates and harvests, so we can compare what the unit hears with what you see.

### Pilot shellfish farms elsewhere in the Adriatic

What you get: the same as the Lim bay farms, one stage later, once the unit has run a season in Lim bay.

What we need from you: the same access and records. Farms in Mali Ston bay, the Novigrad sea and on the Italian and Slovenian coasts would show whether what we learn in Lim bay holds in other water.

### Marine biology and fish behaviour partner

What you get: a new field dataset of seabream activity on working shellfish farms, with synchronised sound and video; co-authorship on the papers that come out of it; a named role, and budget, in the grant applications we write together; the right to set the study design.

What we need from you: a scientist who knows gilthead seabream behaviour and can design the recording study; help confirming the species and labelling feeding events on video; a statistician's eye on sampling and results; your institution as applicant or partner where a grant requires a scientific body.

### Bioacoustics or underwater acoustics lab

What you get: long recordings from a sheltered bay at shellfish farms, and an open-source recording and review system you can reuse.

What we need from you: advice on the hydrophone, its placement and its calibration; access to a calibrated reference hydrophone, or calibration of ours; a review of how we tell feeding sounds from boats, ropes and snapping shrimp. If the project ever tests sound as a response, we need this lab to set the exposure limits and supervise the trials. We will not run such trials without it.

### Embedded and electronics engineer

What you get: paid contract work on open hardware, credited in the repository.

What we need from you: a review of the power, charging and protection design of the surface unit; help moving the ESP32 firmware from host tests onto real boards; bench tests of power draw, heat and brownout behaviour; a board-level design if off-the-shelf parts stop being enough.

### Mechanical and marine engineer

What you get: paid contract work, credited in the repository.

What we need from you: a review of the underwater head, its seals, cable entry and corrosion pairs; loads on the pole or float mount in Lim bay weather; a way to attach the head to a longline that farmers can live with; a plan for pressure and leak testing at a proper facility.

### Permitting and environmental-regulation advisor

What you get: paid advisory work, and a clear brief.

What we need from you: the list of permissions a listening unit needs in Lim bay, which is a special marine reserve, and who issues each one: nature-protection research permission, maritime-domain rules for anything attached to farm gear, any Natura 2000 screening, and data rules for cameras that may film people or boats. Later, the separate route any sound trial would need.

### Manufacturing or marine-hardware partner

What you get: an open design ready for a small batch; first right to build and sell units under the open hardware licence; a design shaped by your production methods rather than redrawn later.

What we need from you: a review of the design for manufacture; quotes for a batch of five to ten units; housings, machining or assembly; advice on service and spare parts.

### Grant co-applicants

What you get: a partner that brings a working software stack, open hardware designs, a pilot site and a written research base, and that will do the engineering and much of the writing.

What we need from you: to apply with us where a call requires a consortium, a farm or a scientific body. Croatia's innovation-in-aquaculture grant under the fisheries fund is open only to aquaculture permit holders and scientific bodies [F1]. Most Horizon Europe and Interreg calls require partners from several countries [F8][F9][F10][F11].

### Angel and impact investors

What you get: an early position in a company working on a measured problem in European shellfish farming, with open designs and a partner network around them. Terms to be agreed.

What we need from you: money for the months grants do not cover, which is most of them, since grants pay late and part-fund; introductions to farms, labs and buyers; patience with a timeline set by fish seasons, not software releases.

### Public grants

What you get: public money turned into open code, open hardware designs, open data where farms agree, and published results, negative ones included.

What we need from you: funding for the stages below. The instruments we are looking at are listed under "Funding routes".

### Stage 3: behaviour trials, only if justified

About 12 to 18 months, and only if stage 2 and a specialist review support it. A controlled trial of whether any response, sound or otherwise, reduces feeding on the lines, with untreated control lines, blind scoring, repeat exposure to check for habituation, and watch on other species. It needs its own permissions. It may end in a result that says no.
